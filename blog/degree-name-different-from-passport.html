Degree Name Different From Passport? Here's What to Check Before Attestation.

The correct solution depends on which document contains the correct information, which authority issued it, and what the destination country's authority will accept. Here's a complete framework for identifying your case.

Short Answer

Do not alter any document. Identify first whether your degree, passport, or CNIC is the correct one. If the degree is wrong, contact the issuing university or board about a correction. If the passport or CNIC is wrong, contact NADRA. Only after the correct record is confirmed should you begin the attestation chain.

Why This Problem Happens

Name mismatches between degrees and passports are extremely common in Pakistan — and almost always have a simple historical cause:

  • Spelling variations — Urdu transliteration differences (Muhammad / Mohammad / Muhammed, or spelling shifts in surnames)
  • Name additions — A middle name, father's name, or family name added at different points in different records
  • Marriage name change — A woman's degree in her maiden name but passport in her married name
  • Clerical errors at the board or university — A typo in the original degree record
  • CNIC vs passport timing — The CNIC was updated but the degree was issued years earlier (or vice versa)

The Critical First Question

Before you do anything, answer this: which record is correct?

If the correct name is in your… Then the fix is…
Passport & CNIC Your degree needs correction or re-issue from the university or board
Degree & CNIC Your passport needs correction at NADRA / DGIP
Degree & Passport Your CNIC needs correction at NADRA
All three are wrong Correct the identity documents first (CNIC → Passport), then the degree

Route 1: Correcting the Degree

If the correct name is on your passport and CNIC, then the degree itself contains an error. The fix depends on who issued it:

For university degrees

  1. Contact your university's examination or records branch
  2. Request a correction or re-issuance of the degree with the correct name as it appears on your CNIC
  3. You will typically need: original degree, CNIC copy, passport copy, an application, and possibly a board/university affidavit
  4. Once the corrected degree is issued, proceed with HEC attestation

For Matric or Intermediate certificates

  1. Contact the relevant Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education (BISE)
  2. Request a correction through the board's official process
  3. Once corrected, proceed with IBCC attestation
Important

Never use tippex, over-writing, stickers, or any physical alteration on a degree or certificate. This permanently invalidates the document and may constitute a legal offence. Always go through the issuing authority.

Route 2: Correcting the Passport or CNIC

If the correct name is on your degree but not your passport or CNIC, you should correct the identity document first:

  • CNIC correction: Visit NADRA with supporting documents showing the correct name (degree, matric certificate, birth certificate)
  • Passport correction: After CNIC is corrected, apply for a new passport through DGIP
  • Processing times vary — allow several weeks before starting attestation

Route 3: Affidavit of "One and the Same Person"

In some cases — particularly with minor spelling variations — an affidavit may be accepted instead of correcting the document. This is a legal statement declaring that the different name spellings refer to the same person.

When it may work:

  • Minor spelling variations (Muhammad / Mohammad)
  • Addition of a middle name that appears in later documents
  • Father's name used as a surname in one document but not another

When it will not work:

  • Completely different names
  • Different father's names that suggest a legal name change
  • Any case where the destination authority requires strict name matching

Even when an affidavit is accepted, it does not change the underlying documents. The destination authority in the receiving country may or may not accept it. This is why the correct route is almost always to fix the source document.

Official Requirement vs. Our Assistance

HEC, IBCC, MOFA, and NADRA set their own rules about what name variations they accept and what supporting documents they require. We do not control those decisions. We help you identify which authority you need, prepare the correct file, and understand what the current requirements are — but the final decision on any document is made by the relevant authority.

What the Destination Country May Require

Even if the mismatch is resolved for MOFA attestation, the destination country's authority — an employer, university, immigration office, or embassy — may have its own requirements. Common patterns:

DestinationTypical requirement
UAEStrict name consistency across degree, passport, and CNIC. Affidavits rarely accepted.
Saudi ArabiaStrict. Mosadaqa and embassy legalization will not proceed without consistent names.
Qatar / Kuwait / OmanStrict — usually requires corrected source documents.
UK / Germany / FranceVaries by institution. Some accept an affidavit, most prefer corrected documents.
Canada / AustraliaGenerally strict on identity documents; may accept a statutory declaration in specific cases.

Always confirm the current requirement with the specific requesting authority — not with a general rule.
