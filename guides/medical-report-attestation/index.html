Medical report attestation

Medical report and fitness certificate attestation for the Gulf: who signs, which attestations come first, and how to prepare a report that is not rejected later.

The short answer

A medical report issued in Pakistan is not accepted directly abroad. It is attested by the issuing hospital or practitioner, then by MOFA Pakistan, and then legalised by the embassy or consulate of the destination country. If the medical examination is carried out inside the Gulf state, it follows that country’s own approved health-centre process instead.

Two different medical processes

There is a difference between legalising a report that already exists and completing a medical examination that a country requires. Employment and residency files usually require the examination to be done at an approved centre. A report you already hold — for insurance, a school, a court or a previous employer — is legalised as a document, and that is where attestation stages apply.

Who signs, and why that matters

The signature and stamp of the hospital or practitioner must be recognisable to the attesting authority. Reports signed by a practitioner whose signature is not on record, printed on plain paper, or issued without a hospital stamp are commonly refused at the first stage. Ask the hospital for a signed and stamped original on letterhead before starting.

The stages in order

  1. Hospital or practitioner issues a signed, stamped original — with the patient’s name exactly as it appears on the passport.
  2. The relevant health authority confirms it where that is required.
  3. MOFA Pakistan attests the document.
  4. The destination embassy or consulate legalises it.
  5. Arabic translation and any further destination-side attestation are added if the receiving organisation asks for them.

Why medical reports are queried

  • Patient name or date of birth differing from the passport
  • Father’s name missing or spelled differently
  • No hospital stamp, no doctor’s signature on record, or a photocopy
  • A report older than the receiving organisation accepts
  • Diagnosis wording or a format the destination does not recognise

How to avoid a second trip

Decide the destination and purpose first, ask the receiving organisation for its written requirement, then prepare one clean original and keep a copy of everything you submit. Corrections belong with the hospital that issued the report, not with the attesting authority.

Questions before you submit

No. This is general preparation guidance. The competent authority and receiving organisation decide what is acceptable.

Yes. Start with a document assessment without uploading a file. An individual review may be needed before selecting a submission route.

Frequently asked questions

Can I attest a medical report I got last year?

That depends on what the receiving organisation accepts. Some require a recent report; others accept an older one once it is attested. Ask first, then prepare the original.

Do you arrange the medical test itself?

No. Required medical examinations are carried out at the approved centres that the destination country designates. We help with the attestation of reports and documents, and with understanding which stage applies.

My report has my father’s name missing — is that a problem?

It can be, because name consistency is one of the first things checked. Ask the hospital about issuing a corrected original and ask the receiving organisation what evidence it accepts for the difference.
